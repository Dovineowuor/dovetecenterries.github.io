Agridoer:
Digitalizing the Agricultural Supply Chain

A comprehensive study of how Dovetec Enterprises transformed fragmented agricultural markets into a unified SaaS platform serving thousands of farmers across East Africa.

Agriculture SaaS Platform E-Commerce Logistics Flutter

01

Problem Statement

The agricultural supply chain in East Africa was fragmented, inefficient, and inaccessible to small-scale farmers who needed a reliable way to connect with urban buyers.

Fragmented Markets

Farmers had no centralized platform to list produce, resulting in wasted harvest and lower prices. Buyers struggled to find reliable suppliers, leading to supply shortages and inflated costs.

Inefficient Logistics

Post-harvest losses reached 40% due to lack of cold storage and coordinated transport. Delivery times were unpredictable, and food safety standards were rarely maintained.

Business Challenge

The client faced a critical inflection point: market dynamics were shifting, and without a digital transformation, their business model was at risk of obsolescence.

Market Dynamics

Declining Market Share

Traditional middlemen were capturing more value, squeezing margins for both farmers and buyers.

Revenue Stagnation

Revenue growth had plateaued at 2% year-over-year, well below industry average of 15%.

Competitive Pressure

Global agri-tech platforms were entering the East African market with superior technology.

The East African agricultural market is worth $50B+ annually, with less than 5% digital penetration. This represents an enormous opportunity for a platform that can bridge the gap between rural producers and urban consumers.

06

User Needs

Derived from extensive interviews and observation, these are the core needs that drove every feature decision.

Discoverability

Farmers need a way to list their produce and have buyers find them easily, without manual searching.

Trust & Transparency

Both sides need verifiable quality standards, pricing transparency, and reliable delivery confirmation.

Speed

Orders must be fulfilled within hours, not days, to preserve perishable agricultural goods.

Mobile-First Simplicity

Most farmers use low-end Android phones with intermittent connectivity. The UI must be lightweight and offline-capable.

07

Features & Functionalities

Every feature maps directly to a user need, validated through iterative prototyping and usability testing.

Smart Marketplace

AI-powered matching of farmers to buyers based on location, product type, quantity, and pricing preferences.

GIS Logistics Tracking

Real-time shipment tracking with cold-chain monitoring and estimated delivery windows.

Secure Payments

Escrow-based payment system protecting both buyers and sellers until delivery confirmation.

Analytics Dashboard

Farmers and buyers access sales trends, pricing insights, and performance metrics.

Offline Mode

Core functionality available offline with automatic sync when connectivity is restored.

08

Product User Challenges

Identified through usability testing and analytics, these are the friction points we systematically eliminated.

Onboarding Friction

Farmers struggled with account creation due to complex forms and verification requirements. Solution: Simplified SMS-based onboarding reduced drop-off by 60%.

Connectivity Issues

Intermittent 2G/3G connections caused data loss and frustration. Solution: Offline-first architecture with intelligent sync protocols.

Language Barriers

Users spoke Swahili, Luganda, and local dialects. Solution: Multi-language support with voice-input and image-based navigation.

Data Entry Burden

Manually entering crop details was tedious. Solution: Barcode scanning and voice-to-text for rapid listing creation.

5 Why Analysis

Root cause analysis of the core problem — low farmer adoption — by asking "Why?" five times.

Why 1

Why do farmers not use digital platforms?

Because they find the existing platforms too complex and time-consuming to use.

Why 2

Why do they find platforms complex?

Because the interfaces require text input and navigation patterns designed for smartphones, not feature phones.

Why 3

Why don't platforms support simpler interfaces?

Because designers assumed all farmers have smartphones with reliable internet and can read text.

Why 4

Why was this assumption made?

Because there was no user research conducted with actual farmers in rural communities before design began.

Why 5

Why wasn't research conducted?

Because the product team was headquartered in Nairobi and had no field researchers in rural farming communities. Root Cause: Lack of empathy-driven research methodology.

Insight

The real problem wasn't technology — it was the disconnect between the design team and the end users. Agridoer's first investment was in embedding researchers directly in farming communities for 3 months before any design work began.

Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

Fishbone diagram analysis of post-launch issues to identify underlying systemic causes.

Problem: Low Order Completion

Fishbone Categories:
  • 🦴 Method: Checkout flow too long (5 steps)
  • 🦴 Machine: Server latency > 3s on payment
  • 🦴 Material: Payment gateway timeout at peak
  • 🦴 Man: Users abandon when asked to create account
  • 🦴 Measurement: No real-time abandonment tracking
  • 🦴 Environment: Network congestion during peak hours
Root Cause: Account creation requirement before checkout
Fix: Guest checkout → 35% increase

Problem: High Churn Rate

Fishbone Categories:
  • 🦴 Method: No onboarding tutorial
  • 🦴 Machine: App crashes on startup (2% crash rate)
  • 🦴 Material: Cache issues on low-storage devices
  • 🦴 Man: Users can't find the search function
  • 🦴 Measurement: No session recording analytics
  • 🦴 Environment: High memory pressure on background apps
Root Cause: No contextual onboarding and discoverability issues
Fix: Contextual tips → 22% retention improvement
